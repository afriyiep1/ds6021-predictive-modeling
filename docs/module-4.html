4.1 Fit multiple predictors together

Multiple regression models the response using several predictors simultaneously:

Yi=β0+β1xi1+⋯+βpxip+εi.Y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_{i1}+\cdots+\beta_px_{ip}+\varepsilon_i.Yi​=β0​+β1​xi1​+⋯+βp​xip​+εi​.

The fitted coefficient bjb_jbj​ is the change in fitted response for a one-unit change in predictor jjj, holding the other included predictors fixed. It is not automatically a causal effect. The insurance example models charges using age, BMI, and number of children.

The design matrix

Put the intercept column and predictors into an n×(p+1)n\times(p+1)n×(p+1) matrix XXX. Then

y=Xβ+ε,y^=Xb.\mathbf y=X\boldsymbol\beta+\boldsymbol\varepsilon,\qquad \widehat{\mathbf y}=X\mathbf b.y=Xβ+ε,y​=Xb.

Minimizing ∥y−Xb∥22\|\mathbf y-X\mathbf b\|_2^2∥y−Xb∥22​ gives gradient −2XT(y−Xb)-2X^\mathsf T(\mathbf y-X\mathbf b)−2XT(y−Xb). Setting it to zero produces the normal equations:

XTXb=XTy.X^\mathsf T X\mathbf b=X^\mathsf T\mathbf y.XTXb=XTy.

If XXX has full column rank, the unique solution is

b=(XTX)−1XTy.\mathbf b=(X^\mathsf T X)^{-1}X^\mathsf T\mathbf y.b=(XTX)−1XTy.

An exact linear dependence makes this inverse unavailable. Highly correlated columns can also make coefficients unstable. The explicit inverse below teaches the formula; numerical least-squares solvers are generally preferable in working code.

What “holding other predictors fixed” means mathematically

The coefficient of BMI in a multiple regression need not match the coefficient from a BMI-only regression. A useful interpretation is to remove from BMI the part explained by the other included predictors, remove the corresponding fitted part from the response, and regress the remaining response variation on the remaining BMI variation. This residual-on-residual slope equals the multiple-regression BMI coefficient when the design has full rank.

If little independent BMI variation remains, estimating its separate association becomes difficult. This explains why correlated predictors can yield stable predictions but unstable individual coefficients. Exact dependence prevents unique coefficients; strong but imperfect dependence increases sensitivity rather than making fitting categorically impossible.

The explicit inverse illustrates the formula when the design has full column rank. Numerical solvers used by modeling libraries are preferable to explicitly inverting XᵀX in general-purpose code.

4.2 Quantify coefficient uncertainty

A fitted coefficient is an estimate and varies with the sample. A pairs bootstrap samples rows of the modeling table with replacement, preserving the response and all predictors together. Refit the same model for each sample.

For coefficient jjj, a bootstrap standard-error estimate is

SE⁡^(bj)=1B−1∑r=1B(bj∗(r)−b‾j∗)2.\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}(b_j)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{B-1}\sum_{r=1}^B\left(b_j^{*(r)}-\overline b_j^*\right)^2}.SE(bj​)=B−11​r=1∑B​(bj∗(r)​−bj∗​)2​.

Use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles for an approximate 95% percentile interval. Interpret its units and conditional meaning. A coefficient interval does not describe where 95% of individual responses lie.

Row resampling assumes rows are appropriate independent sampling units. Keep encoding and predictor definitions consistent across refits. Increasing BBB reduces Monte Carlo noise, but does not remedy a biased sample, dependent observations, or a misspecified model.

Read a bootstrap interval without overclaiming

Suppose an illustrative coefficient interval is 120 to 210 dollars per unit of BMI. The endpoints concern the conditional slope under the specified model and sampling assumptions. They do not say that 95% of individual charges lie in this range, or that a one-unit intervention on BMI causes this change.

The pairs bootstrap treats the observed rows as an empirical population, drawing complete cases repeatedly. A residual bootstrap holds predictors fixed and resamples errors under an assumed error structure. They answer related questions through different approximations; choosing between them requires attention to how the observations arose.

4.3 Connect regression to neural networks

A neuron begins with the same weighted sum used in regression:

z=b+∑j=1pwjxj,h=ϕ(z).z=b+\sum_{j=1}^p w_jx_j,\qquad h=\phi(z).z=b+j=1∑p​wj​xj​,h=ϕ(z).

The weights play a role analogous to regression coefficients; the bias corresponds to an intercept. An activation function transforms the sum. One example is the rectified linear unit:

ReLU⁡(z)=max⁡(0,z).\operatorname{ReLU}(z)=\max(0,z).ReLU(z)=max(0,z).

Stacking only linear or affine layers still gives an affine function. Nonlinear activations allow layers to represent more complex relationships. A multilayer model composes these operations, and gradient-based learning adjusts its parameters using derivatives propagated through the composition.

This connects familiar regression to neural networks without making their parameters equally interpretable. In a network, the effect of one input usually depends on other inputs, learned weights, and active nonlinearities. Greater expressive capacity also increases the need for careful evaluation and control of overfitting.

Why stacking linear layers is not enough

Suppose a first layer computes h=W1x+b1\mathbf h=W_1\mathbf x+\mathbf b_1h=W1​x+b1​ and the next computes y=W2h+b2\mathbf y=W_2\mathbf h+\mathbf b_2y=W2​h+b2​. Substitution gives

y=(W2W1)x+(W2b1+b2).\mathbf y=(W_2W_1)\mathbf x+(W_2\mathbf b_1+\mathbf b_2).y=(W2​W1​)x+(W2​b1​+b2​).

This is another affine transformation. Adding layers without nonlinear activations does not create nonlinear functions of the original inputs. With an activation between layers, that simplification generally fails.

This bridge helps separate two ideas: having many parameters and having a genuinely different function class. Neither automatically establishes better prediction; the next module's evaluation principles still apply.

4.4 Encode categories and fit the pipeline

A categorical predictor must be represented numerically without imposing an arbitrary quantitative spacing. With an intercept and KKK categories, reference coding uses K−1K-1K−1 indicator columns. The omitted category is the reference.

For smoking status, let S=1S=1S=1 for smokers and S=0S=0S=0 otherwise. An additive model is

charges^=b0+b1age+b2BMI+b3children+b4S.\widehat{\text{charges}}=b_0+b_1\text{age}+b_2\text{BMI}+b_3\text{children}+b_4S.charges​=b0​+b1​age+b2​BMI+b3​children+b4​S.

Here, b4b_4b4​ is the fitted smoker–nonsmoker difference at the same values of the other predictors. Including all category indicators and an intercept creates an exact dependence because the indicators sum to the intercept column.

The original pipeline uses one-hot encoding and linear regression. Inspect the actual transformed column names before attaching interpretations to coefficients. Changing the reference category changes coefficient labels and values but not the represented fitted relationships when the model is otherwise equivalent.

Reference coding is a comparison system

For a three-level predictor with reference A, create indicators IBI_BIB​ and ICI_CIC​:

y^=b0+bBIB+bCIC.\widehat y=b_0+b_BI_B+b_CI_C.y​=b0​+bB​IB​+bC​IC​.

The fitted levels are b0b_0b0​ for A, b0+bBb_0+b_Bb0​+bB​ for B, and b0+bCb_0+b_Cb0​+bC​ for C. The B–C comparison is bB−bCb_B-b_CbB​−bC​, not either coefficient by itself. With additional numerical predictors, these comparisons hold those predictors fixed.

Changing the reference category changes the coordinate system for coefficients, not the underlying fitted means. Always inspect the encoder's category order; never infer the reference from the order in which groups happen to appear in a plot.

4.5 Diagnose residuals and interpret fit

Check residuals, not only a fit score

A residual plot should be examined for curvature, changing spread, unusual observations, and dependence. Curvature suggests that the mean function is missing structure. A fan shape suggests nonconstant variance. Time-ordered patterns can indicate dependence. A small training error does not invalidate these concerns.

The classical linear-model assumptions separate different roles: an appropriate conditional mean, independent observations or a suitable dependence model, and constant variance for the usual homoscedastic standard errors. Normal errors support exact small-sample classical inference; normality is not required to calculate OLS.

Explained variation

For an in-sample model with an intercept, define

SST⁡=∑i(yi−y‾)2,R2=1−SSE⁡SST⁡.\operatorname{SST}=\sum_i(y_i-\overline y)^2,\qquad R^2=1-\frac{\operatorname{SSE}}{\operatorname{SST}}.SST=i∑​(yi​−y​)2,R2=1−SSTSSE​.

R2R^2R2 measures reduction in squared error relative to predicting the training mean. It is not the percentage of predictions that are correct and does not establish causality.

With ppp predictor columns excluding the intercept,

Radj2=1−SSE⁡/(n−p−1)SST⁡/(n−1).R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}=1-\frac{\operatorname{SSE}/(n-p-1)}{\operatorname{SST}/(n-1)}.Radj2​=1−SST/(n−1)SSE/(n−p−1)​.

Adding columns to a nested OLS model cannot increase its minimum training SSE, so training R2R^2R2 cannot decrease. Adjusted R2R^2R2 can fall because it accounts for added parameters. Count the encoded columns, including separate indicators. Neither score replaces evaluation on new data.

Separate fitting assumptions from inference assumptions

Question What is needed?
Can least squares compute fitted values? A numerical design and response; rank affects uniqueness of coefficients.
Can coefficients describe the chosen conditional mean? An appropriate mean specification and relevant comparisons.
Are conventional homoscedastic standard errors appropriate? A suitable independent, constant-variance error model.
Can the fit be interpreted causally? Additional design and identification assumptions.
Will predictions generalize? Evaluation on cases representative of the intended use.

A large residual is not identical to an influential observation. Influence also depends on the predictor configuration: a point far from the rest of the design can pull a fitted model strongly. Investigate such observations rather than automatically deleting them.

Book connection: Molnar emphasizes explaining individual terms and category comparisons. Practical Statistics links this interpretation to diagnostics. Use both perspectives before turning an estimated coefficient into a substantive claim.

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import plotly.express as px
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline

# Upload insurance.csv. Begin with the numerical-predictor model.
insurance = pd.read_csv("insurance.csv")
X = insurance[["age", "bmi", "children"]]
y = insurance["charges"]
model = LinearRegression().fit(X, y)
print(pd.Series(model.coef_, index=X.columns))

rng = np.random.default_rng(123)
coefficients = []
for i in range(500):
    rows = rng.choice(len(insurance), size=len(insurance), replace=True)
    bootstrap_model = LinearRegression().fit(X.iloc[rows], y.iloc[rows])
    coefficients.append(bootstrap_model.coef_)

bootstrap = pd.DataFrame(coefficients, columns=X.columns)
print("Bootstrap standard errors:")
print(bootstrap.std())
print("Percentile 95% confidence intervals:")
print(bootstrap.quantile([0.025, 0.975]))
px.histogram(bootstrap, x="bmi", nbins=30,
             title="Bootstrap BMI coefficients").show()

# Extend to categorical predictors; retain all observations for this lesson.
numeric = ["age", "bmi", "children"]
categorical = ["sex", "smoker", "region"]
X_full = insurance[numeric + categorical]
preprocessor = ColumnTransformer([
    ("numeric", "passthrough", numeric),
    ("category", OneHotEncoder(drop="first", handle_unknown="ignore"), categorical)
])
full_model = make_pipeline(preprocessor, LinearRegression()).fit(X_full, y)
y_hat = full_model.predict(X_full)
r2 = full_model.score(X_full, y)
p = full_model.named_steps["columntransformer"].transform(X_full).shape[1]
adjusted_r2 = 1 - (1 - r2) * (len(y) - 1) / (len(y) - p - 1)
print("Training R²:", r2, "Adjusted R²:", adjusted_r2)
fig = px.scatter(x=y_hat, y=y - y_hat,
                 labels={"x": "Fitted charges", "y": "Residual"})
fig.add_hline(y=0, line_dash="dash")
fig.show()

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Why does dropping one one-hot column help when the model includes an intercept?

Reveal explanation

The full set of category indicators sums to the intercept column. Removing one category avoids that exact linear dependence and defines the reference group. Other sources of multicollinearity can still remain.

Optional practice questions

Want more practice? Try these questions after reading the chapter. They are for self-study; work through your reasoning before revealing an answer.

Reveal 5 practice questions

  1. Interpret a reference category

    A model includes an intercept and three indicators for a region variable with four categories. What does the omitted category represent? If you change the reference category, should the OLS predictions change?

    Reveal suggested answer

    The omitted category is the reference: all three indicators are zero for its observations. With other predictors held fixed, each region coefficient compares that region with the reference. Reparameterizing the same model with a different reference changes coefficient interpretations but preserves fitted values, apart from numerical rounding.

  2. Compare fit measures

    After adding a predictor, training R² rises slightly but adjusted R² falls. Is that contradictory? Does either result establish better prediction on new observations?

    Reveal suggested answer

    There is no contradiction. For nested unconstrained OLS models with an intercept fitted on the same rows, training R² cannot decrease. Adjusted R² penalizes additional predictors and can fall when the gain is small. Neither is a substitute for an appropriate out-of-sample evaluation.

  3. Read a residual fan

    Residuals versus fitted values show approximately zero average residual but a much wider spread at large fitted values. Which feature of the model needs attention, and what might happen to an interval method that assumes constant error variance?

    Reveal suggested answer

    The pattern suggests nonconstant conditional error variance, or heteroskedasticity. The mean function may still be useful, but an interval procedure assuming constant variance can have poor conditional coverage: intervals may be too narrow where noise is larger and too wide where it is smaller. Investigate the pattern before choosing an uncertainty method.

  4. Connect regression and a neuron

    A hidden layer forms z = Wx + b, and an output layer takes a linear combination of z. If there is no nonlinear activation between them, what kind of function of x is the result? Why add an activation such as ReLU?

    Reveal suggested answer

    Composing affine transformations yields another affine transformation of x. Stacking such layers alone does not create a nonlinear response surface. ReLU, max(0, z), introduces nonlinearity so hidden units can combine to represent more flexible relationships. Flexibility still needs data and appropriate evaluation.

  5. Interpret a partial association

    In a medical-charge model containing age, BMI, and smoking indicators, the BMI coefficient is positive. Explain what 'holding the other predictors fixed' means. Give one reason this coefficient need not be the causal effect of changing BMI.

    Reveal suggested answer

    It compares fitted mean charges for people differing by one BMI unit while the included age and smoking variables are kept the same. Unmeasured health or behavioral factors may affect both BMI and charges; observational adjustment for the included predictors does not remove all possible confounding. An additive coefficient also assumes the same BMI slope across the represented groups.
